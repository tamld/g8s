# Policy & Epoch Revocation Audit (issue #978)

**Question**: Does revocation / epoch-bump terminate authority for IN-FLIGHT work, or only for new grants?
**Scope**: `issue → dispatch → claim/lease → fsops mutation → verify → deliver`
**Method**: read-only trace of `internal/charter`, `internal/receipt`, `internal/mesh`, `internal/worker`, `internal/fsops`, `internal/controlplane`, `internal/cleanup`, `cmd/g8s/{submit,deliver}`. All paths relative to repo root.

---

## 1. Executive answer

**There is no single revocation propagation rule — there are five divergent ones, and the strongest of them (charter epoch fencing) is not wired into the execution chain at all.** Revocation semantics per layer:

1. **Charter epoch (DELTA-33)**: revoke/epoch-bump terminates *nothing* in-flight and nothing queued, because `charter.Store.Verify` is invoked only by the standalone `g8s charter verify` CLI (`cmd/g8s/charter.go:125`) and the MCP tool (`internal/mcp/tools_ext.go:724`). No dispatch, claim, mutation, or delivery path consults the charter store, and `charter_id` is never stamped onto tasks or receipts (grep over `internal/`, `cmd/`: zero production call sites of `charter.Store.Verify` outside charter CLI/MCP/tests). The doc comment on `RevokeAndReclaim` ("any in-flight S2/S3 operations presenting the old epoch/fence are immediately rejected", `internal/charter/charter.go:608-611`) describes a mechanism that cannot fire: nothing in-flight ever presents an epoch/fence to the store.
2. **Control-plane lease**: revocation (only via `CancelTask` setting `cancel_requested=1`, `internal/controlplane/lifecycle.go:610-663`) *does* terminate in-flight work, but only when the worker's poll loop notices it (default `pollInterval = 200ms`, `internal/worker/worker.go:140`; `internal/worker/worker_runner.go:310-312`) or at completion, fail-closed, via the lease-ownership CAS (`checkLeaseOwnership`, `internal/controlplane/lifecycle.go:433-440`; `FinishAttempt ... AND state='RUNNING' AND lease_owner=? AND lease_token=?`, lifecycle.go:494). This is the **only** operator-reachable in-flight kill switch.
3. **Mesh claim fence (ADR-0038)**: ledger revocation (claim released/accepted/superseded/expired, `internal/mesh/claims.go:137-141,225,270-279`; `internal/mesh/claim_fence.go:32-34`) propagates to in-flight workers at the heartbeat tick — at most every 5s (`heartbeat = clamp(leaseSeconds/3, 100ms, 5s)`, `internal/worker/worker.go:142-143`; reconcile at `internal/worker/worker_runner.go:332-337`), producing `claim_refused_fence` and process-group termination (`internal/worker/worker.go:898`). Bounded-window in-flight termination **works for bound tasks**, but there is **no admin revoke**: `claimReleaseHandler` requires the owner's `node_id+incarnation` (`internal/mesh/claims.go:210-232`), and the global context revision bumps only at claim-accept (`internal/mesh/claims.go:281`) — no operator can drive the stale channel mid-flight.
4. **Write receipts**: manual revoke exists (`RevokeReceipt` deletes the unconsumed row, `internal/receipt/receipt.go:993-1006`) and stops *subsequent mutations* immediately (fsops `VerifyReceipt` → `NotFoundError`, `internal/fsops/claim_fence.go:25-31`), but never kills the child process, leaves **no tombstone** (revocation is indistinguishable from never-issued, and a same-scope receipt can be silently re-minted), and is impossible **after consumption**: once `ValidateAndConsume` succeeds at attempt end (`internal/worker/worker_sweep_collect.go:739-759`), `deliver` needs only the stored `AllowedPaths` (`cmd/g8s/deliver.go:368-383` consumed-fallback, `:390-445` scope match) — the post-consume, pre-delivery window is **unrevokable**.
5. **Capability flags** (`G8S_TIER=F1`, `G8S_PERMISSION`, `G8S_RECEIPT_ID`, `G8S_SCOPE_ROOT` env stamps at spawn, `internal/worker/worker.go:783-793`; argv sanitization `:796`): grant-time immutable, never re-derived, cannot be revoked in-flight at all. Backstop is the post-run diff firewall (`VerifyWorktreeDiffScopeWithOptions`, `internal/worker/worker_sweep_collect.go:702-711`).

So: **epoch-bump (charter) is new-grants-only in theory and no-grants in practice; TTL expiry bounds everything; manual revoke exists and terminates in-flight work at exactly one layer (control-plane cancel) plus one stop-mutation layer (receipts).** No layer's revoke cascades to any other layer's artifacts (e.g. `CancelTask` neither releases the mesh claim nor revokes the receipt; `RevokeAndReclaim` touches neither leases nor receipts).

## 3. Divergence analysis and gap classification

**Divergence verdict: N=5 incompatible invalidation models** — (a) epoch/fence comparison (charter), (b) DB CAS + poll (lease), (c) tx-revalidated projection + heartbeat reconcile (mesh claim), (d) row-delete + per-mutation Verify (receipt), (e) immutable env stamps (capabilities). They share no propagation bus: revoking one artifact never revokes artifacts derived from it (receipts are clipped *from* claims at issue time, `receipt/claim_fence.go:69-102`, but claim release does not revoke clipped receipts; charters are parents of nothing).

### G1 — Charter enforcement is unwired; `RevokeAndReclaim` doc claims in-flight rejection that cannot happen — **exploitable (authority theater)**
`internal/charter/charter.go:608-611` promises "any in-flight S2/S3 operations presenting the old epoch/fence are immediately rejected". No production code path presents epoch/fence: `cmd/g8s/submit.go:92-94` checks only `CurrentStratum()` adjacency (env-derived, `charter.go:102-115`); `internal/controlplane/store_claims.go:100-210` has no charter input; `internal/fsops/fsops.go:359-366` checks only stratum + scope; `cmd/g8s/deliver.go` has zero charter references. An S2 operator who revokes a charter has zero mechanical effect on tasks already queued or running under that mandate. Exploitability: a compromised/rogue S2 keeps dispatching until charter TTL expiry (≤24h) with no revoke remedy other than infrastructure-level (DB) intervention.

### G2 — `deliver` accepts delivery after claim/lease revocation — **exploitable**
The only mesh-fence re-check happens inside worker collect before consumption (`internal/worker/worker_sweep_collect.go:702-710`). Between attempt completion and `deliver`, an operator may cancel the task or the mesh claim may be released/re-scope-shifted; `deliver` (`cmd/g8s/deliver.go:344-445`) validates only the receipt projection. The consumed-receipt fallback (`:370-383`) even skips `ClaimBinding.Validate` because it reads the row via `ListReceipts` and uses only `AllowedPaths` (`:390-392`). Result: authority revoked at T2 still lands at T3. (Mitigating: git HEAD CAS at `:474-520` protects against concurrent target drift, not against authority loss.)

### G3 — fsops delegated mutations never consult the mesh ledger — **exploitable, bounded window**
`internal/fsops/claim_fence.go:12-13` states this is by design ("reads only the receipt projection, never the mesh ledger"). Ledger revocation reaches fsops only indirectly: harness heartbeat reconcile (`worker_runner.go:332-337`, ≤5s) terminates the attempt. Exposure window after a mid-flight ledger release is therefore ≤ one heartbeat + poll skew, **but only if the binding was not renewed immediately before the release** — `RenewClaimBinding` re-signs a fresh ceiling with the renewed lease expiry (`receipt/claim_fence.go:143-148`), so a release one tick after renewal leaves a binding valid for up to the old lease's remaining life while fsops happily accepts it (`fsops/claim_fence.go:35` validates the projection, not the ledger).

### G4 — No operator-reachable mesh revocation — **theoretical (missing control surface)**
The mesh's stale channel (`E_CLAIM_FENCE_STALE`) is the fastest in-flight terminator, yet nothing administrative can trip it: release is owner-scoped (`internal/mesh/claims.go:210-232`), `context_revision` bumps only inside `claimAcceptHandler` (`claims.go:281`), and there is no revoke/amend handler that flips another actor's claim state. A coordinator who can revoke charters and receipts cannot revoke claims — the layer with the best propagation has the worst control surface.

### G5 — Receipt revocation leaves no tombstone; post-consume authority is unrevokable — **exploitable (deliver window), theoretical (cohort revoke)**
`RevokeReceipt` is a physical DELETE (`internal/receipt/receipt.go:993-1006`): no audit row, no revoke-reason, and re-minting an identical-scope receipt afterward is indistinguishable from the original authority (fresh UUID, fresh ceiling). Because receipts carry no epoch, cohort revocation ("kill every receipt derived from charter X / claim Y") requires enumerating IDs via `ListActiveReceipts`. And after `ValidateAndConsume` (`receipt.go:874-989`), no revocation path exists at all — exactly the state `deliver` relies on (`cmd/g8s/deliver.go:370-383`).

### G6 — Capability flags cannot be demoted in-flight — **theoretical / by-design for short attempts**
`G8S_PERMISSION`, tier, and scope env are stamped once per attempt (`internal/worker/worker.go:783-793`). There is no re-derivation from a policy source; the stratum firewall (`fsops.go:363`) trusts env, which `CurrentStratum` clamps only against F1/F2 spoofing (`charter.go:102-115`). A policy change mid-attempt (e.g. "no more commits") cannot reach a running child; the backstop is the post-run diff firewall, which attests but does not prevent.

### G7 — No cascade: each revoke is local — **by-design today, but it is the root cause of G1–G5**
`CancelTask` (`lifecycle.go:610-663`) touches only the tasks row — not the receipt, not the mesh claim, not the charter. `RevokeAndReclaim` (`charter.go:611-632`) touches only the charter row. `RevokeReceipt` touches only the receipt row. `claimReleaseHandler` touches only the claim row. Four revocation buttons, zero shared semantics, zero cascade.

### G8 — Cleanup is not authority-aware — **by-design (GC)**
`CleanupWorktrees` sweeps by branch-pattern + age + cleanliness (`internal/cleanup/worktrees.go:117-243`); it neither consults leases/claims nor revokes anything, and protects dirty worktrees (`:195-203`). No gap beyond G7's general rule: a revoked task's committed worktree is swept on age alone, and a live task's worktree can be swept if it goes quiet past `--older-than` while clean.

### Guard rails that already work (pin them)
- Lease CAS refuses stale completion (`lifecycle.go:433-440`, `:494`).
- Receipt monotone ceiling forbids DB-side expiry extension (`receipt.go:603-625`); renewal extends only after ledger renewal and never shortens scope (`receipt/claim_fence.go:110-160`).
- Single-use consume is cross-process atomic (`receipt.go:965-978`); replay by another task is a hard worker failure (`worker_sweep_collect.go:740-758`).
- RefuseClaim releases a claim without burning attempt budget (`store_claims.go:42-82`).
- Charter `Verify` does reject superseded epoch/fence — at its (unwired) check points (`charter.go:599-604`).

## 5. Answer to the headline question, restated

Revocation today is **layer-local and mostly new-grants-only**: the only mechanisms that terminate in-flight authority are (1) `CancelTask` → `cancel_requested` → worker poll (≤200ms) → process-group kill, and (2) mesh claim staleness detected at the ≤5s heartbeat reconcile — and (2) has no operator trigger while (1) never cascades to the artifacts the child actually presents (receipt, claim, env stamps). The charter epoch fence — the mechanism explicitly designed to invalidate in-flight authority — is validated nowhere in the execution chain, making `RevokeAndReclaim`'s in-flight-rejection comment (`internal/charter/charter.go:608-611`) currently a documentation claim without a mechanism. Receipts stop *future mutations* on revoke (row delete) but neither kill the child nor survive consumption, leaving the consume→deliver window permanently unrevokable.

**Gap count**: 2 exploitable authority-theater/deliver gaps (G1, G2), 1 exploitable bounded-window gap (G3), 1 exploitable unrevokable-window gap folded into G5, 3 theoretical/missing-control-surface (G4, G5 tombstone aspect, G6), 2 by-design (G7 as root cause, G8). Eight red-test proposals: 7 RED, 1 GREEN guard (RT-978-H).
